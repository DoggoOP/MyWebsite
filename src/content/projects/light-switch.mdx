## Retrofit mechanism

I designed this prototype to operate an existing wall switch mechanically, without changing the building wiring. The CAD model uses a **lead screw and guided carriage** to move the actuator across the switch. That layout lets me work out the travel, mounting clearance, and alignment before building the housing.

## Control prototype

The wall-mounted prototype places the actuator beside the switch. An **ESP32 board** and push button are wired on a breadboard for control testing; the wiring is still exposed at this stage. The photo documents the physical arrangement and test setup, rather than a finished enclosure or a verified phone, voice, or scheduling feature.

<figure>
  <img src="/assets/light-switch-prototype.jpg" alt="Wall-mounted light-switch actuator wired to an ESP32 and push button on a breadboard" loading="lazy" />

The next mechanical checks are repeatable switch travel, end-stop behavior, and mounting stiffness. Those tests matter because the actuator has to move the switch reliably without loading it beyond its normal travel.
