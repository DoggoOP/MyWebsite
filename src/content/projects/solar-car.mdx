## Engineering work

I built **AutoCFD**, a pipeline that accepts a vehicle CAD file, sets up a Luminary Cloud simulation, monitors the run, and extracts drag, side force, lift, center of pressure, and convergence information. The application also logs results to Google Sheets so geometry and wind cases can be compared. The code includes farfield and mesh setup, optional rotating-wheel treatment, and a job dashboard. These are implemented workflow features, not claims that every simulation has been validated experimentally.

The same workflow can run **Shellpower**, a solar-array layout and simulation tool, on an exported car-body mesh. It places cells under a 6.0 m² target-area constraint, evaluates exposure and occlusion across sampled headings and race hours, and estimates peak power and daily energy. The current code supports a symmetric layout and an optional shadow-aware comparison run. I worked on bringing aerodynamic and solar estimates into one run because a sail can affect both drag and array shading.

[AutoCFD source](https://github.com/DoggoOP/SolarCarLuminaryAutoCFD)

## Adaptive retractable sail

This study tests a retractable, auto-cambering sail for crosswind energy harvesting. The proposed mechanism changes camber and trim when apparent wind is favorable and stows when the combined drag, rolling resistance, shading, and control costs exceed the aerodynamic benefit. The mechanism remains a design study. Deployment logic, durability, control power, and race integration still need validation.

## Working assumptions and decision checks

One working route scenario assumes a 3 m/s southeast wind during the first 40% of the race to Alice Springs. The noted relative-wind vector is (-26.71, -2.12, 0) m/s, giving a magnitude of about 26.79 m/s and an angle of about 4.54°. This is a scenario for analysis, not a verified race forecast. The current energy estimate of **0.88 kWh/day gained** and **0.20 kWh/day lost** to shading and drag is also preliminary; its underlying wind frequency, electrical conversion, and deployment assumptions need a documented calculation.

The sail also changes side force, tire load, and roll moment. The notes include a 260 kg vehicle mass, a 550 N maximum side-force case, and a reported center of pressure for one AC75 case at (-0.9, 0.41, 0.31) m relative to the center of the front wheels at ground level. Those inputs need a common coordinate frame and a tire-load model before a rollover or steering conclusion. The initial actuator estimate is 16.25 N·m of boom torque under a 130 N side load; gearing and holding-power claims still require a mechanical test.

The next study needs a control map over apparent-wind speed and direction: when to deploy, what sail angle of attack to command, and how to adjust camber. Lift-to-drag ratio is one aerodynamic measure, but the useful objective for the car is net race energy after side-force rolling losses, shading, and actuator power. Lead-car anemometer data could supply a wind distribution for that calculation. Before production, the mechanism also needs repeatable trim and retraction tests, structural and fatigue checks, mass and packaging limits, and a failure-safe stowed state.
