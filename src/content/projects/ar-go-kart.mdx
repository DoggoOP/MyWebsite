## A physical kart on a virtual course

We built an augmented reality go-kart around a **real, drivable kart**. The game, **TDX Cart**, is a mixed reality kart race inspired by Mario Kart. The rider walks or drives a loop once to record the course, and a Meta Quest 3 lays a glowing rainbow road over the real ground with coins and items along the road. Picking up an item changes how the real kart drives. A coin, for example, raises the kart's top speed by 4%.

## Building the kart

We **sourced the parts**, **designed the electrical system**, and **welded the frame** ourselves, so the kart, the wiring and the AR game were built as one project.

## Connecting the game to the throttle

We **intercepted the throttle signal** between the foot pedal and the motor controller. A Raspberry Pi sits in that path. An ADC reads the pedal, the Pi combines the pedal position with a speed multiplier from the headset, and a DAC writes the result to the motor controller. The headset adds up the active boosts and streams the multiplier to the Pi 20 times a second over UDP, through a Wi-Fi hotspot hosted on the Pi. On the bench, full pedal gave about 2.47 V at a multiplier of 1.00 and ramped smoothly to about 2.95 V at 1.30.

Safety is handled at both ends. The app always runs in passthrough mode, hides virtual content whenever tracking is uncertain, and sends a multiplier of zero while tracking is lost, so the kart gets no motor power. If the headset stops sending, the Pi returns the throttle to neutral, and the kart also has a physical kill switch.

As of October 2026, the full chain from foot pedal through the Pi to the motor controller runs with boosts on the bench. A hardware watchdog and the first full ride are next.
